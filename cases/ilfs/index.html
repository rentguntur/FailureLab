FAILURE LAB · CASE #009

IL&FS: When the group became too complex to see the risk

IL&FS did not collapse because one infrastructure project suddenly failed. The deeper problem was a highly leveraged, multi-entity system whose long-term assets, short-term funding and internal financial links became harder to see — until a few defaults turned a liquidity problem into a system-wide confidence shock.

1987–2026Leverage · liquidity · group complexity · governanceSystemically important finance

01 · The business

Infrastructure Leasing & Financial Services (IL&FS) grew into a large infrastructure-finance and project-development group. It financed, built and invested in roads, tunnels, power, water and other infrastructure through a network of subsidiaries, associates and special-purpose entities.

What it did
Raised money and deployed it into infrastructure projects and financial services.
What made it attractive
Long-lived infrastructure assets could generate value over years, while lenders supplied funding along the way.
Hard constraint
Debt had to be serviced on time even when project cash flows were slow, delayed or trapped inside subsidiaries.

03 · Business Model Flow

Raise debt / capital
→
Fund projects & subsidiaries
→
Wait for project cash / monetisation
→
Service group debt
→
Refinance / recycle capital
Failure point: this model can work only while refinancing, asset monetisation and project cash flows remain dependable. Once lenders stop extending time, a long-term asset portfolio can become a short-term liquidity crisis.

04 · Why it looked safe

IL&FS was not an obscure startup. It was a systemically important non-deposit-taking finance company with major public financial institutions among its investors and creditors. The group had built visible infrastructure assets and carried investment-grade ratings before the crisis.

That matters because the warning problem was not simply “nobody knew the company was leveraged.” The harder question was whether the full group could be understood as one connected balance sheet with one liquidity risk.

05 · The first serious warning

In June 2018, IL&FS Transportation Networks delayed repayment of about ₹450 crore. Rating downgrades followed. By August and September, defaults spread across group entities and instruments. India Ratings cut IL&FS to IND D on September 18, citing deterioration in liquidity and missed debt-service obligations.

Failure Lab reading: the first useful warning was not the final downgrade. It was a mismatch between obligations due soon and cash that could actually be mobilised soon.

06 · What the record established

The Government of India said in October 2018 that the group had 169 companies in 2017–18, negative operating cash flow and a net cash outflow of ₹7,020 crore during that year. It also described excessive leverage and a deep mismatch between debt and equity. The government superseded the existing board and appointed a new board.

The later NCLAT status report gives an even clearer picture: as of October 2018 the group comprised 302 entities, with estimated external debt outstanding of about ₹99,377 crore. Four key holding companies accounted for almost 51% of external fund-based debt and on-lent substantial amounts within the group.

Important distinction: the complex structure itself was not automatically fraudulent or doomed. The Failure Lab conclusion is that complexity became a risk multiplier because it made liquidity, guarantees, inter-company dependence and entity-level weakness harder to assess quickly.

07 · 10-Minute Failure Scan

Economics
Project returns may be positive over years while financing costs arrive monthly. Ask whether each major asset can support its own financing burden.
Cash
Map cash by entity. Cash inside a subsidiary is not automatically cash available to the parent.
Balance sheet
Track debt maturity dates, guarantees, letters of comfort, cross-defaults and refinancing dependence.
Operations
Identify projects where cash collection depends on government claims, concessions, termination payments or long delays.
Growth
Do not treat a larger asset portfolio as safer if it requires proportionally more refinancing.
Management information
Ask: if the group lost access to new borrowing for 90 days, which entities would miss payments first?

Reinforcing loop: default → rating downgrade → funding becomes harder → asset sales become urgent → less flexibility → more concern from lenders → more pressure on liquidity.

11 · Decision Gates

Gate 1 — Before new borrowing
Show debt-service capacity under a refinancing freeze, not just the base case.
Gate 2 — Before group expansion
Map guarantees, inter-company loans and cash dependencies created by the new entity.
Gate 3 — At first default
Treat the event as a group-level liquidity signal until proven otherwise.
Gate 4 — Before asset sale
Test whether selling one asset solves the liquidity problem or simply moves value between creditors and entities.

12 · Could it have been stopped?

Probably earlier — but not with one magic ratio. The intervention window was the period when leverage, refinancing dependence, project delays and entity-level cash constraints were becoming visible before the 2018 cascade.

The practical counterfactual is a group-wide liquidity control system: consolidate all debt maturities, guarantees, inter-company exposures and genuinely transferable cash into one stress model. Then impose a hard limit on new borrowing when the downside case fails.

That is a Failure Lab counterfactual, not a claim that one control would certainly have prevented the crisis.

14 · Current status

IL&FS has not simply vanished. The original group is being resolved through asset sales, transfers, monetisation and other mechanisms. In its July 2026 status filing, IL&FS reported that as of June 30, 2026, ₹50,387 crore had been discharged to creditors against a resolution target of ₹61,000 crore. It reported resolution of 205 of 302 entities, with moratorium protection still required for 41 entities.

So the accurate description is: the original structure entered a government-led resolution and restructuring process; many assets and entities have been resolved, but the process is still ongoing.

15 · Final Failure Tree

Root conditions
Long-gestation infrastructure + high leverage + complex multi-entity structure.
Reinforcing mechanism
Refinancing dependence + delayed project cash + inter-company funding + loss of lender confidence.
Terminal event
Defaults spread, ratings collapsed, liquidity tightened and government intervention became necessary.
Pattern added to the Failure Lab library: complexity can hide liquidity concentration. A group can look diversified because it has many companies and projects while remaining dependent on the same small set of funding channels.

16 · Tomorrow Test

If you were starting this business tomorrow, what would you check so you don't repeat this mistake?

Start with one page: every entity, every debt maturity, every guarantee, every inter-company loan, and every rupee of cash that is actually transferable. Then ask the uncomfortable question: “If nobody lends us another rupee for 90 days, who fails first?”
