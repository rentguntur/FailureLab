Case #005 · Marketplace · Travel

Stayzilla: when growth hid the cash problem

Stayzilla did not fail because nobody wanted alternative stays. It failed because the company moved from a model that generated cash to one that required heavy spending on both sides of a young marketplace — while the scorecard increasingly rewarded growth metrics instead of cash flow and working capital.

Working capitalMarketplace liquidityGrowth disciplineFunding dependenceShutdown & insolvency
The core lesson: A marketplace can look bigger every month while becoming harder to fund. If demand and supply must both be created, growth is not free — and GMV is not cash.
01 · THE BUSINESS BEFORE THE FAILURE

What was Stayzilla?

Stayzilla started as an online accommodation marketplace, initially focused on budget hotels and later moving heavily into homestays and alternative stays. By early 2017 it claimed more than 55,000 stay options across about 4,500 towns. It had raised roughly $33–34 million.

Customer

Travellers looking for hotels, homestays and alternative accommodation.

Supply

Small hotels, homestays and other accommodation providers, including in smaller towns.

Revenue

A commission-based marketplace: earn when a booking happens.

Sources: Economic Times · University of Manchester research

The hidden cost: In a mature marketplace, one side can pull the other. Stayzilla said homestay demand and supply were still absent in many locations, so it had to spend on both sides — creating hosts and creating guests.
04 · WHY IT LOOKED INVESTABLE

Scale was visible. The cash engine was less visible.

Real achievements

Stayzilla built a large national network, moved beyond budget hotels into homestays, and worked with tourism bodies. The company also invested in technology for demand forecasting, price prediction and booking-confirmation prediction.

The dangerous scoreboard

Founder Yogendra Vasupal later said the company had shifted attention toward GMV and room-nights and away from cash flow and working capital. That is a founder's retrospective explanation, not an independent causal finding — but it is consistent with the marketplace economics he described.

Sources: Founder post-mortem · Business Standard

05 · FINANCIAL SNAPSHOT

Growth accelerated. Losses accelerated faster.

The reported FY15–FY16 numbers make the warning visible: revenue grew strongly, but losses and marketing spend grew much faster. These figures are a financial snapshot, not a complete reconstruction of cash flow.

₹4.2 Cr → ₹13.8 Cr

Reported revenue, FY15 → FY16

₹19.6 Cr → ₹94.5 Cr

Reported loss, FY15 → FY16

₹11.2 Cr → ₹56 Cr

What matters: revenue increased about 3.3×, while reported losses increased about 4.8× and marketing spend about 5×. That does not by itself prove marketing caused the failure; it shows that growth was becoming substantially more expensive than the revenue growth it produced.

06 · TIMELINE

From cash-positive roots to a funding-dependent marketplace.

2005–2012

Inasra is built around online accommodation. The founder later described the first seven years as a period of negative working capital, positive cash flow and internally funded growth.

2010

Stayzilla becomes the consumer-facing brand as the company develops its accommodation marketplace.

2014–2015

The company pushes aggressively into scale and alternative stays. Business Standard reported 20,000+ properties across 1,200 locations in 2015.

2015–2016

Travel competition and discounting intensify. Stayzilla invests heavily in both sides of the homestay marketplace and expands across India.

May 2016

Series C funding of about $13.5M is reported. The company continues building the homestay network.

Feb 23–24, 2017

Stayzilla suspends new bookings, honours near-term bookings and announces a planned reboot with a different business model. A large team is let go.

Sep 15, 2017

NCLT admits insolvency proceedings against Inasra Technologies following a vendor claim.

Aug–Sep 2018

NCLT orders liquidation; IBBI records Inasra as defunct, with admitted claims of ₹10.81 lakh and no resolution plans.

07 · THE MONEY JOURNEY

The model changed before the headline did.

EARLY MODELCustomer booking → commission → cash inflow. Founder says working capital and cash flow were healthy.
MARKET CREATIONNeed hosts + guests in places where neither side was dense.
GROWTH SPENDMarketing, marketplace teams, supply creation and discounts absorb cash before commissions arrive.
FUNDING DEPENDENCEMore growth requires more capital. If new investors do not arrive, the operating model loses room to breathe.
Failure Lab analysis: The key transition was not simply “losses increased.” It was a change in the cash architecture of the business: from a model that could fund growth through working-capital advantages to one where marketplace development required substantial upfront investment.

Founder Vasupal said the travel marketplace lacked local network effects: Stayzilla could not simply win one city, establish liquidity, and then move to the next. The supply-demand problem was geographically fragmented.

09 · THE METRIC TRAP

Growth can improve the dashboard while weakening the business underneath.

WHAT RISESGMV, room-nights, listings, towns and headline network size.
WHAT CAN HIDECost to activate supply, cost to create demand, local liquidity and repeat behaviour.
WHAT DETERIORATESContribution, cash conversion and the ability to fund growth internally.
THE DECISIONStop asking “Are we bigger?” and ask “Does each increment of growth make the business stronger?”
Failure Lab interpretation: GMV is not useless. Room-nights are not useless. They become dangerous when they are allowed to substitute for the financial metrics that determine whether the marketplace can keep operating.

11 · WHAT COMPETITION DID — AND DID NOT DO

Competition was an amplifier, not a complete explanation.

What the evidence supports

Stayzilla operated in a market where large travel platforms and later accommodation specialists increased pressure on customer acquisition, pricing and visibility. Competition made an already capital-intensive marketplace harder to fund.

What we should not claim

It is too simple to say “OYO beat Stayzilla.” The founder's own post-mortem focused on the company's changing financial discipline and the cost of creating supply and demand. Competition belongs in the causal chain, but it should not replace the underlying business-model analysis.

12 · THE SMALLEST USEFUL UNIT

Don't measure the marketplace only in room-nights.

Per booking

Commission earned minus payment processing, support, discounts and booking-linked service costs.

Per location

Bookings generated from a town versus the full cost of building and maintaining supply and demand there.

Per cohort

Repeat booking and contribution from acquired travellers versus the cost to acquire and activate them.

Control rule: A marketplace should have a clear answer to “If we stop adding capital, which locations or customer cohorts still generate cash?”
13 · WARNING SIGNALS

What should have triggered a stop-and-review?

1. Growth metrics outrun cash metrics

Signal: GMV, room-nights and listings rise while operating cash generation deteriorates.

Action: Board review of contribution and working-capital bridge every month.

2. Each new town needs fresh demand creation

Signal: No repeatable local liquidity playbook.

Action: Set a town-level payback gate before expansion.

3. Funding becomes part of the operating model

Signal: Base plan assumes another round to sustain ordinary operations.

Action: Maintain a no-new-funding survival plan and a hard cash runway floor.

4. Vendor obligations become fragile

Signal: Disputed or overdue invoices begin threatening continuity.

Action: Maintain an aged-payables dashboard and a formal dispute/settlement process.

15 · WHO SHOULD HAVE ACTED?

Different people owned different warning signs.

Management

Keep cash flow and working capital beside growth metrics; stop geographic expansion when unit-level liquidity fails.

Board / investors

Ask what happens if the next funding round is delayed by 12 months. Growth plans should survive that question.

Finance / operations

Make overdue vendors, refunds, receivables and cash runway visible before they become shutdown problems.

16 · COULD STAYZILLA HAVE BEEN SAVED?

Possibly — but not by simply raising more money.

This is counterfactual analysis, not a historical fact. More capital could have extended runway, but the founder's own account suggests the core problem was that the marketplace required heavy investment to create liquidity and had moved away from its earlier cash-flow discipline.

Earlier intervention

Freeze expansion into low-density towns. Focus on a smaller set of locations where supply and demand can become liquid without continuously buying both sides.

Possible decision rule: reopen expansion only after a cohort of locations demonstrates repeatable contribution and improving cash conversion.

Harder intervention

Redesign the business around positive contribution and working-capital discipline, even if that means becoming a much smaller company.

Possible decision rule: choose a smaller business that can survive without assuming another funding round.

17 · STAKEHOLDER IMPACT

The failure did not end with the app.

Employees

The February 2017 shutdown meant a large team was let go; contemporary reports put the workforce around 200–210 at the time.

Vendors

Payment disputes became central to the shutdown aftermath. The Jigsaw claim was ₹1.69–1.72 crore in contemporary reports; the parties disputed the underlying invoice.

Investors

About $34M had been raised. Existing investors later described the final funding as a last attempt to keep the company afloat.

Important distinction: NCLT later admitted insolvency on the basis of a vendor claim. That legal outcome does not prove that the vendor dispute caused the original operating failure. The operating model had already been suspended months earlier.
18 · CURRENT STATUS

The original company is in liquidation.

What is verified

Inasra Technologies entered CIRP on 15 September 2017. NCLT ordered liquidation on 26 September 2018, and IBBI records the company as defunct with no resolution plans.

As of 2026, NCLT records still show proceedings connected to the company, while the corporate debtor remains in liquidation.

What not to say

Do not say “Stayzilla disappeared because of one unpaid advertising bill.” The consumer business had already been suspended in February 2017. The later vendor dispute and insolvency process were part of the aftermath.

Sources: IBBI insolvency table · NCLT case record · Business Standard

19 · FAILURE TREE

20 · TOMORROW TEST

If you were starting this business tomorrow, what would you check so you don't repeat this mistake?

Before opening a new market

Can one town reach liquidity without permanently subsidising both sides?

What is the expected payback period for supply creation and customer acquisition?

Gate: expansion requires a defined path to contribution-positive local economics.

Before raising the next round

Can the company survive 12 months without new funding?

Which metrics are cash metrics, and which are only activity metrics?

Gate: the base plan must include a survival case with no new funding.

Every month

What is contribution per booking, per town and per customer cohort?

How much cash is tied up in receivables, refunds and unpaid vendor obligations?

Gate: review contribution, cash conversion and working-capital movement by town and customer cohort.

When the numbers deteriorate

Who has authority to freeze expansion?

What exact threshold forces the company to shrink rather than raise?

Gate: assign an owner and pre-agreed trigger for freezing expansion.
