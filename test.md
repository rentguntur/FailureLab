And importantly: near-failures

A company doesn't have to completely disappear.

Some of the most useful cases may be:

"They almost failed. Here's what went wrong, what they noticed, and what they changed."

That can actually be more useful for a business owner, because the goal isn't to study dead companies. The goal is to learn how to recognize trouble before the business dies.

This also changes how we research

We shouldn't simply search:

"famous companies that failed"

Instead, we should continuously look for interesting failure mechanisms.

For example:

Case	Business size	Main lesson
Dunzo	Startup → large	Growth vs unit economics
Small restaurant	Small	Sales ≠ profit
SaaS startup	Startup	Customer acquisition economics
Manufacturer	Medium	Working capital
Retail chain	Growing	Expansion
Large corporation	Large	Debt/governance
Family business	Small/medium	Succession
Near-failure startup	Startup	Early warning + correction

That makes Failure Lab much more powerful.

The company is the story.
The failure mechanism is the lesson.
The warning sign is what the reader should learn to spot.

That should remain the foundation of the site.
